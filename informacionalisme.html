SE-08 · Framework

Informacionalisme

Knowledge, power, and the struggle for truth across civilizations

Framework · book in progress · 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

An ancient pattern with a new name

Scholars of democratic backsliding have described authoritarian informationalism: the manipulation of information flows by modern regimes. The phenomenon is real, but it is not new. Informacionalisme — named in the French register for its aristocratic genealogy — is the systematic replacement of operative knowledge with managed ignorance, calibrated to a literacy ceiling.

Three pressures, every era

Knowledge drifts and degrades without active preservation. Its custodians develop interests that diverge from truth. And power manufactures legitimating narratives. The pattern holds from the scribal monopolies of Mesopotamia and Egypt to Semmelweis, whose proof that handwashing saved mothers was rejected because accepting it was intolerable. What has always ended it, imperfectly and for a time, is the written record and the education that makes it legible.

Gresham’s law applied to truth

The internet was supposed to collapse the literacy ceiling. Instead counterfeit signal, cheaper to produce and richer in emotion, drove out good. Flooding an epistemic economy is the same maneuver as flooding a currency: the aim is not to win a transaction but to destroy the medium of exchange.

Commissioned intelligence

Artificial intelligence is a misnomer. Intelligence has always been commissioned: Augustus commissioned Virgil, the Church commissioned Michelangelo, crowns and popes founded the universities. What is new is the scale, the speed and the invisibility of the patron. The question is who commissioned the intelligence, toward what end, and what corrective signal it was built to suppress.

The framework also explains what the study of ignorance usually misses: why people cling to their own misinformation. Ignorance is not only inflicted from above. It is cultivated from below, when an institution offers the security of managed ignorance in place of the discomfort of knowing. Once ignorance is known to be remediable, choosing to remain in it is a moral failure.

Informacionalisme is the subject of a book in progress. Working papers on the framework, including its relation to agnotology and to institutional mimicry, are available on request.
