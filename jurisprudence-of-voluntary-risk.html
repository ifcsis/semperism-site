The Jurisprudence of Voluntary Risk

When institutions may penalise lawful private conduct

Outline · October 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

Liberty follows the person; liability follows the risk

Lawful private conduct that carries risk — smoking, cannabis use, motorcycling, firearm ownership — is treated one way by an insurer, another by an employer, another by a civil service, and another again by the military. The variation is large, and it has no author.

An accidental jurisprudence

Insurance regulation, employment law, civil-service rules and military policy each settled their own corner of the question. Taken together they amount to a body of law no one set out to write, and it does not answer to a single principle.

The test

The outline proposes that an institution may act on lawful private conduct only where it can show an institutional interest, a nexus between the conduct and that interest, and a response proportionate to the risk — and it separates inquiry from action, since asking about conduct and penalising it are different acts.

A further distinction does much of the work: between risk a person creates and carries, and risk a person externalises onto others.

The revenue trap

A state that licenses and taxes a vice acquires a fiscal interest in its consumption. Legalisation therefore changes the institution’s position as well as the citizen’s, and the outline treats that shift as part of the question.

The full outline works through the paired hard cases, risk pooling, the objections, and an open research checklist.
