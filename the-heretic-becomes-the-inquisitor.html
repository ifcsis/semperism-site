The Heretic Becomes the Inquisitor

Institutional mimicry under terminological displacement

Essay · sixth version · August 2026 · S. Harrison Knudson, J.D.

The puzzle

The secular critique of religion was accurate: orthodoxy enforced by social pressure, dissent punished by excommunication and shunning, sacred objects that put power beyond challenge, belief performed as a loyalty test. The same inventory now describes enforcement in institutions that reject religious authority entirely, under different names. Why?

Not a church, a closed institution

The usual answer is that the movement is a religion, or descends from one. This essay takes a third route. The mechanisms were never religious in origin. They are what any institution does when it must hold a boundary without recourse to evidence. Religion is the best-documented instance, which is why the mechanisms carry religious names.

The unit is the institution — a department, a newsroom, a party committee, a licensing board — because only institutions can expel, decertify or dismiss. Six observable criteria mark how closed an institution is, by degree rather than kind.

Why the words change

No committee replaced excommunication with cancellation. The cheaper descriptions displaced the expensive ones, and three pressures did the selecting: critique-insulation (the old words hand opponents a century of argument), legitimacy transfer (therapeutic language carries a presumption of evidence that theology does not), and participation cost (a recruit will join a process that holds people accountable who would never join an inquisition).

The pattern repeats, and cuts both ways

The French Terror, Soviet show trials and Maoist struggle sessions each denounced orthodoxy and rebuilt its architecture. The essay applies the same test against the grain, to the Republican Party’s censure and non-recognition of its own dissenters after January 2021, and finds the same structure under a different lexicon.

The vocabulary changes. The structure does not. And the new vocabulary is not a disguise applied afterward; it is the condition on which ordinary people keep taking part.

The full essay situates the argument against McWhorter, Bottum, Lifton, Durkheim, Garfinkel and Kuran, sets out the six criteria and the substitution table, and states three conditions that would count against it.
